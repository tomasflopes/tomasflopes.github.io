The Sunday league

I have always liked football. The year I turned 18 I stopped playing in a club, and started playing with my friends on Sundays instead. It turned into our own little Sunday league.

That gave me the idea for my final high school project: a shared platform where everyone in the group could interact and save the stats of every match, to make it a bit more competitive.

A mobile app in a course that taught none

My high school course never taught anything about mobile apps, so I taught myself. I built the app in React Native, with a Node.js backend and a MongoDB database behind it: groups of friends, invites, matches and a leaderboard.

It was the first time I touched so many new technologies in a real project. Until then I had only seen them in free, guided online bootcamps.

The parts only I knew about

Two things in it were just for me: automated tests, and file uploads stored in an S3 bucket. Nobody asked for either. I read about them somewhere online and decided to go for it, and it went great.

The presentation went great too. I got 20 out of 20, as a student who built a mobile app in a course that didn't teach it. I even forgot to mention the tests and the S3 bucket while presenting.

Looking back

I'm proud of it for what it was: a big challenge, in an era when AI wasn't a thing.

Two things make me laugh. The design is awful. It was my first time using Figma and my first time doing any design, loosely inspired by apps I found on Dribbble. And the architecture: the code is really messy and makes zero sense.

I never published it or used it in real life, and I abandoned it after the presentation. I made myself a promise that one day I'll bring it back.
